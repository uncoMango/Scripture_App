Free preview — Matthew 6:33

Below is a sample word study from the collection. The entry examines a single Greek word in Matthew 6:33 that most English Bibles translate as "seek" — but whose range of meaning goes further than that.

Matthew 6:33 — Koine Greek source
"But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you."
ζητέω zēteō G2212
to seek · to crave · to demand

Most English translations render zēteō as "seek," which implies a calm, deliberate searching. The Greek carries more weight than that. The word appears across the New Testament to describe a craving, a pressing demand, or an insistence — not a passive looking.

In Matthew 6:33, the present-tense imperative form means something closer to "keep on craving" or "make this your constant pursuit." The Kingdom is not something you look for when you have time. Jesus is describing the orientation of a whole life.

The same root appears in Luke 12:29–30, where Jesus contrasts what the nations "run after" (epizēteō) with what his disciples are to pursue — the same insistence, redirected.

About the author
Kahu Phil Stephens
Author  ·  Biblical Scholar  ·  Pastor

Kahu Phil Stephens is a Native Hawaiian pastor and graduate of Kamehameha Schools. He has spent 30 years studying the Bible in its original languages — Biblical Hebrew for the Old Testament and Koine Greek for the New Testament.

He served 8 years in pastoral ministry on Molokaʻi, working within Native Hawaiian communities where the gap between translated scripture and its original meaning had direct consequences for how people understood their identity and calling.

The word studies in this collection are drawn from that work — not academic exercises, but years of pastoral reading in the original text.
